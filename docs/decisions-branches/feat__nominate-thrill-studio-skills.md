## 2026-08-18 03:13 - A skill may ship references/, under four tests — and empirical-design-principles now passes all four

**Reasoning:** PR #233 nominates five skills and is the first in this catalog to ship a `references/` subdirectory, so it sets the precedent for every later one. The adversarial panel ruled MERGE WITH FIX on a defect that shows exactly why the precedent needs tests: the skill's `description` promised seven principles — Gestalt/common region, Fitts's, Hick's, Miller's, Jakob's, von Restorff, aesthetic-usability — and the body delivered five. Measured, with a control: `fitts` and `jakob` occurred 1× in the frontmatter and 0× in the body, while the same probe returned 2 for `gestalt` and 1 each for `hick`, `miller` and `restorff`. Both were fully covered in `references/`, which is the trap: an agent that loads only SKILL.md — and the consuming reviewer loads only SKILL.md — got the promise of Fitts and Jakob and none of the substance, including the correct movement-time direction and the Jakob scope caveat. The subdirectory had become a place content went to disappear.

So the ruling is not "references/ is allowed" but "references/ is allowed if it carries source material rather than instructions", made checkable as four tests:

1. **SKILL.md stands alone.** Everything the reader must act on is in the body. `references/` carries provenance, primary quotations and the audit trail — the things a reader consults, not the things they apply.
2. **Nothing the frontmatter promises lives only in references/.** The description is the trigger surface; a principle named there and delivered only in a reference file is a broken promise to a reader who never sees the file. Probe it by counting each promised term in the body, and control the probe against a term you know is there.
3. **The on-disk relative path is the primary link.** `npx skills add` copies subdirectories wholesale, so an installed reader already has the files; an absolute `blob/main` URL sends them to the network for a file sitting next to them, and promises a path never moves. Absolute URLs are SHA-pinned or dropped.
4. **The subdirectory is never a byte-budget escape.** Relocating instruction prose to get under validate_skills.py's 8192-byte cap deletes it for the reader the gate exists to protect. That is distinct from shipping sources, and the gate's own error message now says so instead of banning both.

**Alternatives considered:** Ban `references/` outright and force every skill into one file — rejected: the three reference files here carry ~1,250 lines of primary-source verification (Fitts's own equations, Palmer's page-numbered hedges, the Tesler archive retrieval) that no reader needs in-band and no 8192-byte body could hold; banning it would have deleted the evidence rather than relocating it. Raise SIZE_LIMIT so the material fits in the body — rejected for the reason already recorded at validate_skills.py:65-84: the cap tracks what the consuming reviewer actually reads, so raising it does not make a longer body reach anyone. Leave the description promising seven and let `references/` cover two of them — rejected, that is the defect. Trim the description to the five the body delivers — rejected: Fitts and Jakob are the two most-cited laws in interface design and the two this catalog most needs to correct, so dropping them from the trigger surface loses the skill its main job.

**Implications:**
- `skills/empirical-design-principles/SKILL.md` now delivers Fitts and Jakob in the body, in the front half (byte 3597 of 8130; a consuming reviewer truncates, so content past the midpoint is written but not received). Both carry the direction statement and the scope caveat, not just the name: Fitts as "rises with distance, falls with target size, logarithmically", scoped to a target of known location and to a pointer that can pin an edge; Jakob scoped to control placement and behaviour, with the visual half marked as Nielsen's opinion.
- Body is 8130 bytes, 62 under the cap. Room was made by dropping the three `blob/main` URLs (−300 bytes, measured) and by trimming three passages that restated something the file already said elsewhere — a heuristics enumeration duplicated in the frontmatter and in Cross-references, a two-line summary of the paragraph above it, and a Cross-references gloss repeating its own first clause.
- The verification claim at the end of that skill no longer asserts completeness. It states the scope instead: the three reference files hold 78 source entries, about 20 were opened and read against the original, and the rest are DOI- or URL-resolved only — a resolving DOI shows a work exists, not that it says what the citing line claims. A skill about evidence cannot be the artifact that overclaims its own.
- **The first draft of that very sentence carried the defect it exists to fix, and the catch is worth recording.** It cited the audit's "31 of 31 DOIs resolve" and "seven NN/g URLs". Those are whole-PR numbers. Counting distinct DOIs per scope with a probe control-tested both ways — non-zero for a DOI visible in the file, zero for a fabricated `10.9999/` prefix — gives 31 across all five skills this PR adds but **12** inside `empirical-design-principles/references/`, and 6 nngroup.com URLs rather than seven:

  ```sh
  grep -rhoE '(doi:|doi\.org/)?10\.[0-9]{4,9}/[^ ,;)*"]+' skills/empirical-design-principles/references \
    | sed 's/[.,]$//' | sed -E 's#^(doi:|https?://(dx\.)?doi\.org/)##' | sort -u | wc -l
  ```

  A reader takes a number in a skill's Sources section as describing that skill. Carrying a PR-wide figure there is the same scope drift the audit caught in Yin et al. and Peterson & Salvagio — inherited from the report rather than measured in place. The rule this makes concrete: a number entering a permanent artifact gets re-measured at the artifact's own scope, however reliable the source, and the sentence now uses a count the reader can reproduce against the files it names.
