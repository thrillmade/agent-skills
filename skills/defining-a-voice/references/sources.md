# Sources: defining-a-voice

Why [defining-a-voice](../SKILL.md) and [voice-template](../../voice-template/SKILL.md) hold what they hold: the research trail for maintainers. The skill states its rules as the catalog's own opinion; this file records which sources informed which rule, verbatim, so each can be re-checked. Every quote was read at its URL on 2026-11-05. Section names refer to the skill.

Licence note: the Mailchimp Content Style Guide is CC BY-NC 4.0. The skill borrows its structure (traits as bounded pairs, a tone map) and quotes one line with attribution; its trait pairs and examples are the catalog's own.

## Precedence

Seven guides, listed below, put clarity above personality, and none of the guides read for this skill puts it the other way.

- Mailchimp, [Voice and tone](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/): "Mailchimp’s tone is usually informal, but it’s always more important to be clear than entertaining." (the line the skill quotes)
- Monzo, [Tone of voice](https://monzo.com/tone-of-voice): "we never want our sense of humour to get in the way of the core message."
- Atlassian, [Voice and tone](https://atlassian.design/foundations/content/voice-tone): "All of our content should be practical at a minimum."
- Apple, [Writing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing): "Prioritize clarity and avoid the temptation to be too cute or clever with your labels."
- GOV.UK, [Clear language](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/): "Plain English is mandatory for all of GOV.UK."
- Microsoft, [Brand voice](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/brand-voice-above-all-simple-human): "Our voice hinges on crisp simplicity."
- NN/g, Moran, [The Impact of Tone of Voice on Users’ Brand Perception](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-voice-users/) (2016): "don’t let your attempts at humor get in the way of actually communicating to users the information they need to know."
- Shared base versus brand layer: Amy Hupe, [Structuring documentation for multi-brand design systems](https://amyhupe.co.uk/articles/structuring-documentation-multi-brand-design-systems/) (2022): "even when brands are stylistically very distinct, there are a lot of baseline principles and rules that don’t need to change." Intuit, [AI voice and tone](https://contentdesign.intuit.com/ai/voice-tone/): "This guidance doesn’t replace product-level voice and tone guidelines."

## Voice and tone

- Mailchimp, [Voice and tone](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/): "You have the same voice all the time, but your tone changes."
- Atlassian, [Voice and tone](https://atlassian.design/foundations/content/voice-tone): tone "should change depending on the situation the user is in (for example, receiving an error versus successfully completing a project)."
- Keying tone to the situation rather than to a guess at feelings: Shopify Polaris, [Voice and tone](https://web.archive.org/web/20231221131020/https://polaris.shopify.com/content/voice-and-tone) (archived 2023-12-21): "The reality is we never know a person’s emotional state." Mailchimp, Atlassian and NN/g key tone to the reader's emotional state; the skill keys it to the situation and keeps the likely state as a note.
- Neutral as severity rises: Intuit, [Errors](https://contentdesign.intuit.com/product-and-ui/errors/): "Severe errors … Convey the message in a neutral tone to avoid escalating an already-tense situation."

## What a guide contains

- **Traits as bounded pairs.** MailChimp, [Voice and tone, 2016 capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20170119174812/http://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/): "One way to think of our voice is to compare what it is to what it isn’t." Polaris (archive above): "Be real, but not too tough or overly familiar". Intuit (above): "Be conversational but not verbose."
- **Tone map as dials.** Monzo (above), by channel: "Marketing comms … Everyday magic: Medium Warm wit: Medium". Atlassian (above), per trait: "When to be less bold — Person is feeling: apprehension, confusion, annoyance, fear, anger."
- **Four scales.** NN/g, Moran, [The Four Dimensions of Tone of Voice](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-of-voice-dimensions/) (2016): "There are four primary tone-of-voice dimensions"; "It would be unusual for a writing style to be placed at the outermost limits of the tone dimensions and still be effective for business purposes." Anti-tone words: "Consider choosing anti-tone words."
- **Trust over friendliness** (stated as a lean, not a law). NN/g, [tone-voice-users](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-voice-users/): "On average, 52% of the variability in the desirability scores is explained by trustworthiness. Friendliness only explains an extra 8%". The samples were small (100 American respondents; effects of 0.3–0.7 on 5-point scales), so the skill states this as guidance.
- **Humour off by default.** NN/g (above): "Humor is extremely risky, because when it fails, you annoy and alienate your users." (the line the skill quotes) NN/g, [Error-Message Guidelines](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/error-message-guidelines/) (2023): "Avoid humor since it can become stale if users encounter the error frequently." Google, [Set the right tone](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/error-messages/set-tone): "Don't attempt to make error messages humorous." Monzo (above): "We want people to feel part of the joke, not the target of it." Atlassian (above): "Once may amuse, but a dozen times may annoy." On translation ("won't survive translation"): Atlassian (above), "question whether it will be understood or appreciated by our global audience"; Google (above), "Users can misinterpret humor. (Jokes don't always cross borders well.)" The 404 carve-out (only beside the way out, never in place of it; never in an error that blocks a task) is the catalog's ruling: a page that still gives the next step keeps every clarity rule.
- **Pronouns stated outright, because guides split.** Apple (above): "Avoid using we altogether because it may be unclear who the “we” in question refers to." GOV.UK, [Right tone](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/right-tone/): "make sure you’ve already used the full name of your organisation before you use ‘we’."
- **Vocabulary lists.** Apple (above): "Create a list of common terms, and reference that list to keep your language consistent." Monzo (above): "Avoid empty adjectives & marketing cliches".
- **Exceptions and owners.** Mailchimp, [Writing legal content](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/writing-legal-content/): "all legal content either starts with or passes through the Legal team."
- **Testing with readers.** NN/g, [Tone-of-voice words](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-voice-words/): "Give the samples to actual users, and ask them to decide which tone words best describe each of your content pieces".

## Voice checks

- **Shouting.** GitHub Primer, [Content](https://primer.style/foundations/content): "In most cases, exclamation marks are not appropriate — most things aren't *that* exciting." MailChimp, [Failure message, 2015 capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20150225004624/http://voiceandtone.com/failure-message/): "Don’t use exclamation points or alarming words". GOV.UK, [A to Z style guide](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/style-guide/a-to-z): "DO NOT USE BLOCK CAPITALS FOR LARGE AMOUNTS OF TEXT AS IT’S QUITE HARD TO READ."
- **Register.** Apple (above): "Interjections like “oops!” or “uh-oh” are typically unnecessary and can sound insincere." Intuit, [Celebrations](https://contentdesign.intuit.com/content-patterns/celebrations/): "If we celebrate too often, and for minor accomplishments, celebrations could become annoying, or worse, condescending".
- **Pronoun drift** and the review format came from brand-voice-review §7 and its "How to phrase findings", which carried them as catalog opinion.
